## 4. Subject line and hook patterns

1. **Contrarian/myth-kill:** "wellness ROI doesn't exist", "There is no longevity certification", "AI is making wellness more valuable", "Nobody asked for your next wellness certificate."
2. **They don't buy X, they buy Y:** "Companies don't buy wellness retreats, they buy offsites."
3. **Big precise number:** "$25 billion entered wellness in six months", "Companies pay $9,600 a year for a weight loss pen", "wellness has 1.2 billion new customers", "58% of the wellness economy sits in five countries."
4. **Numbered list or system:** "3 wellness markets nobody owns yet", "5 wellness roles AI is making more expensive", "the 4 rungs...", "Five documents decide..."
5. **Buyer's quote as subject (objection):** "'Send me a proposal' usually means no", "'we already have an app for that'."
6. **Confession one-liner (first person, lowercase):** "I didn't have a business, I had a calendar", "i worked 3 weeks for free and never heard back."
7. **"You" accusation:** "your headline is pricing you", "your certificates will not get you a wellness role", "why nobody calls you an authority (yet)."
8. **Hidden/insider:** "The biggest paycheck in wellness is hiding", "The wellness price list companies never publish", "The wellness buyer no one is pitching yet", "I know a person between you and the wellness budget."
9. **Shift/news:** "Who pays for wellness just changed", "this summer has split wellness in two", "Companies are about to stop paying for this."
10. **Analogy:** "Loneliness is the new smoking."
11. **Q&A series prefix:** "Can I pick your brain: [question]?"

**Preheader trick:** the preview text always adds a second hook ("this objection is a confession," "4 contracts, 4 signers, and none of them sit in HR," "the thinking is the product. you keep sending it for free").

## 5. Email structure template

Length is about 900 to 1,400 words free. Tone: first-person, warm, slightly cheeky, Spanish-flavoured, short paragraphs and one-line punches, with heavy use of "amigos" and the rhythm "Here is... Now look at... Read that again."

1. **Italic "In today's brief"** summary (2 to 3 lines, promises the outcome and the paywalled tool).
2. **"Hola amigos" plus a scene opener** of 5 to 10 lines from his life that same day (Marbella, La Coruña, the Dubai heat, coffee at McDonald's, the kids, his father), bridged to the business lesson ("That is the whole lesson of today's newsletter").
3. **"By the end of this brief, you will have:"** three bullets, the last one being the named framework.
4. **Section 1, "The Shift / The Misread":** "Here is how most of this industry reads X... That is wrong."
5. **Section 2, "The Evidence":** 3 to 5 sourced numbers (GWI, Gallup, Business Group on Health, Mercer, KFF, McKinsey and others), each one interpreted.
6. **Section 3, "The Budget Translation":** always "Run it through the only question that matters: what budget line does this sit on?" This section names the buyer by title, the metric they are measured on, and a **"Stop saying '...' / Start saying '...'"** swap.
7. **A free tactic** ("One free move before the paywall, ten minutes"). Examples: the Aftercare Test, the LinkedIn age audit, the Bridge Question, the Permission Audit, the headline test.
8. **A governing question** for the reader ("which of the 3 checks would you pass?").
9. **"Below the line:"** bullets of the paid assets: a framework, word-for-word scripts, pricing and 2 to 3 AI prompts. Then the price, the urgency and the value math.
10. Mid-email promo block ("⚠️ This block only shows to some of you") for the Skool community or "The Desk."

The Pick My Brain emails run 150 to 300 words: a reader question, a short numbered answer, a "Your one-minute action," "reply with your question," and an opt-out P.S.

## 6. Corporate-wellness selling frameworks (free portions)

### 6.1 The master rule
"What budget line does this sit on?" is asked in almost every issue. Wellness sold as "wellbeing" lands on the HR engagement/perks line: "the first one a CFO trims," "the smallest pool with the longest approval chain." The aim is to move to performance lines (operations, L&D, risk, benefits, claims, continuity) and name a metric the buyer already reports.

Stop "We will improve employee wellbeing and morale." Start "We will move a specific metric you already report: absence days, regretted attrition, or manager-driven engagement, and here is how we will measure it."

### 6.2 Who the buyer is (by line)

| Offer/problem | Budget line | Signer | Measured on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic sessions/workshops | Event/engagement (HR) | HR/wellbeing manager | (weakest line) |
| Manager-layer work | L&D: manager capability, leadership development, first-line manager training (17 Jul). Later he argues for the **health-cost line** because leadership development is "small, discretionary, first to be cut" (18 Sep) | Head of L&D under the CHRO; COO; CFO + Head of Total Rewards | Pipeline strength, team output, cost per employee, medical trend |
| Postpartum/return-to-work | Retention | CHRO/Head of Benefits | Attrition of returning mothers |
| Maternal mental health | Healthcare benefits and claims | Benefits lead + insurer | Claims |
| Menopause/45+ healthspan | Senior talent retention; occupational health/insurance; continuity | CHRO; benefits; ops | Senior attrition, claims |
| Heat/climate readiness | Facilities; Legal & Risk; business continuity; benefits at insurance renewal | Head of Facilities; General Counsel; COO; Head of Benefits | Duty of care, output |
| Offsites/retreats | Offsite/events/leadership budgets ("leadership offsite Q1," "sales kickoff," "exec alignment, two days") | Chief of Staff, COO, CRO, Founder (HR is cc'd) | Decisions made, output |
| Wellness real estate | Capex at design stage + Opex after handover | Developer | Delivering the 10 to 25% "wellness premium" |
| GLP-1 / drug claims | Pharmacy and claims ("offset line") | Benefits lead/CFO | Claims share (11.4%) |
| Compliance dates | Legal/ops/HR compliance | Owner of the statutory date | Being defensibly ready |
| Cost-shift softener | Total rewards | CHRO | Visible wellbeing while deductibles rise |
| Scan/diagnostic aftercare | Clinical service lines; executive health screening | Clinics; employers | Renewal rate |

**Typical SME approver (100 to 400 people):** the HR manager or general manager, often under the committee threshold. **HR's personal risk:** "If they bring in a supplier who cannot show anything, their name is on it." The CFO or COO above them asks, "what does the company get and how will anyone know?"

**The listkeeper (30 Sep):** big companies buy from a list of about 3 names kept by procurement or the **insurance broker** (Mercer or WTW at large firms, local brokers at small ones). The broker earns 2 to 10% of the insurance bill (up to 50% on some extras), so "your $10k programme is pocket money." What the broker needs is client retention. WTW: 88% of 232 employers will change suppliers within 2 years (55% for wellbeing). BGH: 51% are reviewing wellness vendors. Decoder:
- "Let me check with our broker" means you are not on the list.
- "Can you register on our supplier portal?" is good news; do it the same day.
- "Send me your pricing" with no meeting means you are the fourth column in a spreadsheet; the cheapest wins.
- Response script: *"Of course. Who do you check with? Happy to send them what they need directly, so it's less work for you."*

### 6.3 Pricing
- **Price sets your "pile"** (14 Aug):
  - A few hundred dollars is petty cash, the lunch talk nobody remembers.
  - A few thousand with a clear result is a job that one manager owns.
  - A monthly fee with a number attached is a budget line that gets reviewed and renewed.
  - In his words: "A $15,000 problem gets a $900 answer."
- **Never quote one number. Quote a range ("Range Return"):**
  - Stop "I charge $150 an hour," "about $900 for the workshop," "I can work with your budget."
  - Start "Work like this runs $8,000 to $20,000, depending on what we include."
  - Script: *"Good question. It depends where the work sits. A review like the one we talked about runs $2,000 to $5,000. If it becomes something ongoing, that is a monthly arrangement, and I would build it around the number you mentioned. One thing that helps here: what can you approve without asking anyone else?"* Then stay silent ("whoever speaks first loses").
  - Cut scope, never the rate. HBR/McKinsey: a 1% price rise gives an 11.1% profit rise.
- **3 units companies pay in** (23 Sep): per session (event budget), per head (benefits budget, PEPM) and per claim dollar (health-cost budget). Worked example:
  - 25 people at a lunch session in a 600-person company = $300, once.
  - The same expertise as a 12-month back-care programme for all 600 at **$3 PEPM = $21,600/yr** on the benefits line, renewed on utilisation.
  - Tie it to MSK claims and it moves to the third line, priced against cost avoided.
  - Benchmark for the floor: an EAP costs about £4.50/head/yr (a UK council, £1,800 for about 400 staff).
- **4 rungs** (14 Sep): (1) hours to individuals, (2) a month to individuals, (3) a project to an organisation, (4) a day of judgment to an organisation. "Nearly everyone who thinks they are on 3 is selling 2 with better wording."
  - Consultant benchmarks: $150 to $200/hr; day rate 6 to 8x the hourly; average US management-consulting day about $1,800; UK independents £950 to £3,000/day; 120 to 160 billable days a year.
  - Math: $1,800 x 130 days ≈ $234k versus $80/hr x 20 hrs x 46 weeks = $73,600. Same knowledge, 3x the money.
  - Three of the four rate factors can change "this quarter"; the fourth takes a year (details are paywalled).
- **Hourly ceiling** (9 Sep): 25 to 30 billable hours a week; $80 x 30 x 46 = $110,400 gross; 20 hours = $73,600; US trainer median $46,180 (BLS). "I didn't have a business, I had a calendar." The fix is a retainer. Clients already get access and judgment for free (menus on Friday night, a knee flaring on Saturday), so charge for it.
- **Fractional ladder** (29 Jul): day rate, then retainer, then a seat. First role-holders are paid "from the problem's budget, not headcount."
- **Scarcity pricing** (31 Aug): in-person and small-group work rises in value as digital tends to $0. "Never discount against a $19 app."
- **Heat deals** are "priced above the unadapted market."

### 6.4 Entry offers, pilots, paid diagnostics
- **Never free.** "Free assessments get ignored. Paid ones get read." "Nobody has a free scan to see whether the radiologist is any good." Free proposals cost 6 to 10 hours each; at a 1-in-4 win rate that is 30 to 40 unpaid hours per contract, or $2,400 to $3,200 at $80/hr. "You give away the scarce thing (diagnosis) and charge for the replaceable one (sessions)."
  - Give value as **questions** that make the buyer see the cost of the opportunity, the cost of delay and the cost of doing nothing.
- **Small, paid, with a door** (3 Oct): a small price gets a faster yes (less risk, fewer people, often no procurement). Write the next step on the same page as the price. Agree success criteria in the contract so the renewal case is obvious. Exercise: write the entry offer in one line: what they get, the price, what it leads to.
- **Proof Pilot for new markets** (19 Aug): stop "I have ten years of experience and I am new to this market"; start *"I run a six-week pilot on one metric your company already tracks, and you keep the data either way."* Six weeks keeps any mistake small. One tracked metric means sick days, attrition on one team or engagement. A fixed fee avoids procurement. "You keep the data" means they win regardless.
  - Pick the smallest room that can say yes fast, not the dream client.
  - The pilot is never free; a discount is traded for **testimonial rights**.
- **Prevention test** (4 Oct), three steps:
  1. Pick a cost they already measure (sick days, claims, quits, overtime).
  2. Find the leading indicator (bad sleep, untaken holidays, midnight emails, low energy).
  3. Run a **paid 12-week test on one team of 20 to 50**: measure the leading sign weekly and the cost at the end, against a control team. Agree success up front.
  - Pitch line: *"Sick days in your [team] are [X]% above the market average this year. Give me 12 weeks with them. We'll track sleep every week and sick days at the end, next to a team I don't touch. The data is yours, whether we keep working together or not."*
- **Manager pilot:** "A priced pilot for 20 managers, reported against one cost line."
- **Manager Multiplier / Manager Load Stack:** three tiers from a 90-minute diagnostic to a structural retainer, plus a 12-question Manager Load Audit with scoring bands (paywalled).

### 6.5 The pitch and the meeting
- **"Send me a proposal" usually means no** (7 Aug). The buyer decides in the first 3 to 5 minutes. Presenters remind them of "the app nobody opened, the workshop everyone clapped for." "The buyer is not skeptical of you; they are skeptical of the last three people who looked like you."
  - Questions win for three reasons: they calm the buyer's real fear (approving another failure), they find the number (attrition, absence, claims, survey score), and they let the buyer convince themselves.
  - Leave every meeting with three things: the cost in their numbers, the budget line and the signer's name.
  - Stop "Let me show you what we do." Start **"Walk me through what you've already tried."**
  - The full Thirty-Minute Diagnosis is paywalled: an opener, 4 questions, a say-it-back and 2 closing asks.
- **Contract Chain, the "5 documents"** (10 Aug). Score 1 point for each link you could produce today; most readers score 2.
  1. **Know who's paying** (pick the offset, compliance or softener line).
  2. **Move in the quiet** (booked September conversations).
  3. **Arrive with proof:** the **Judgment Memo**, two pages: the problem, what everyone gets wrong, what I'd do first. "Three people receiving it is a distribution strategy."
  4. **Run the meeting as a diagnosis.**
  5. **Convert the small yes:** a two-week review, a one-department look or a data pull as an "evidence engine."
  - "Contracts are not won, they're assembled."
  - Stop "I'm waiting to hear back." Start "The review gets to their desk Friday, and the decision is whether to scale it."
- **Role Proposal one-pager** (29 Jul): name the problem in the company's numbers, the role that owns it, and the smallest paid step. "New jobs are not posted, they are proposed."
- **Entry Sentence / headline:** describe the problem you solve, not your job title.
  - Stop "certified breathwork and resilience coach." Start **"I run heat-season readiness programs for operations teams"** (8 Jul).
  - Headline test (16 Sep): send your headline to anyone who has signed a purchase order and ask "What do you think I sell?" If they name your job, you are in the sessions pile. If they name a problem, you are in the right pile. "Not sure" is the most expensive answer.
  - GWS 2026 language: companies are moving from "stress reduction" to **"capacity protection."**
- **Authority, three checks a budget owner makes** (26 Aug): what you show (a service menu or a judgment about their problem), where they've seen you (wellness rooms or business rooms), and what you ask for (hours or a mandate with a metric). The Authority Ninety (paywalled) runs in three phases: build the artifact, change the room, change the ask.
- **Permission Audit** (21 Sep):
  1. What am I waiting to have (one noun)?
  2. Who, by job title, would ask for it?
  3. What has a paying client already got from my work that I could count?
  - A blank Q2 means a permission with no issuer. A blank Q3 is the real gap: "4 weeks of counting."
  - Real exceptions: insurance, a licence, background checks.
  - Data: 78% of employers rate experience as equal to or more valuable than a degree; 86% value certificates (WGU 2025).
- **Warm pipeline: the Bridge Question** (17 Aug). Never pitch a client; respond when they open the door ("hectic day at work"). Script: *"You've mentioned the team a few times. Out of curiosity, who owns that problem at [company]? Not asking for an intro. I work on this from the company side and I'm always trying to understand how different places handle it."* Move it to a post-workout drink. The Client Ledger (paywalled): score your book by title, company and recurring complaint; ask for the introduction; send a page within 24 hours.
- **Openers that skip the convincing:** the heat deal (28 Aug): *"Your risk committee logged heat this year for continuity reasons. Nobody has walked the same exposure across your workforce. I do that in ten working days, and you get a document Facilities can act on and Legal can file."* Other examples: "This category is already funded in the US and the UK. I run it here." "I build recovery and load management into your existing manager development program." "I design and operate the health programming that delivers your wellness premium."
- **Manager stop/start table** (18 Sep):
  - "A resilience program for all staff" becomes "A capacity pilot for the managers who set team engagement."
  - "Improve wellbeing scores" becomes "Protect output in the teams where manager load is highest."
  - "Boost employee engagement" becomes "Reduce manager exit risk and the team attrition behind it."
  - "Per-head pricing across the company" becomes "A priced pilot for 20 managers, reported against one cost line."
  - Arithmetic: a workshop reaches 200 employees once; 20 managers reach those 200 daily.
  - Health-cost math for a 500-person company: $17k per employee = $8.5M; +9.5% ≈ $807,500 of new cost.

### 6.6 Objection handling
- **"We already have an app for that"** (4 Sep). It is a confession: they bought "column one," the work that can be written down. Don't compare features and don't offer "an app with a human touch." Ask *"Good. What has changed since you rolled it out?"* and wait.
  - If they name a number that improved, find what the app doesn't cover.
  - If they name activity (downloads, sign-ups), they were never asked the second question.
  - If they go silent, that is the same answer.
  - Position: "You are never the alternative to the app; you sit next to it and own the number the app was supposed to move." There are two dashboards, app usage versus attrition, claims and engagement, and the second is why they took the meeting.
  - Paywalled: responses for EAP, points platform, meditation app and AI coach.
- **"Send me a proposal":** see 6.5. The paid answer is to sell a Paid Diagnostic instead.
- **"There's no headcount":** "Correct and irrelevant." First role-holders are paid from the problem's budget (retention, occupational health, L&D).
- **"It's too expensive":** scripted, but behind the paywall (Rate Architecture). The free principle: cut work, never the rate.
- **"Let me check with the broker":** ask who they check with (6.2).

### 6.7 ROI versus "VOI"
He never uses the term "VOI." His alternative to ROI (21 Aug) works like this:
- "3.7x ROI in 12 weeks" claims die with the CFO because ROI is a methodology claim that needs a constant environment. Real workforces change: people leave, insurance reprices, leadership changes.
- He says you can't convincingly show a wellness intervention works in under 3 years: "trend lines, not headlines."
- He borrows from longevity clinics: pick 3 biomarkers, measure them quarterly, claim **direction, not attribution.**
- Corporate version: a **3-year directional commitment on 3 CFO metrics**: voluntary attrition replacement cost, healthcare claims-loss ratio, and productivity hours lost. The CFO is measured on **cost-trend predictability.**
- Milestones: Year 1 directional movement, Year 2 mid-trend confirmation, Year 3 the cohort trends below benchmark, with an audit checkpoint at each annual review.
- Governing question: "Which of the CFO's existing line items does this trend affect?"
- Supporting evidence:
  - RAND/PepsiCo: disease management returned $3.78 per $1; the lifestyle component didn't pay back.
  - Oxford (Fleming 2024, 46,336 workers): individual programmes (mindfulness, resilience, apps) showed no benefit. "Change the workplace, not just the worker."
  - Measurement instruments he sells as entry points: the Manager Load Audit (12 questions), the Workplace Healthspan Audit (12 questions, 4 domains), a "LinkedIn age audit," and "price the exits": last year's 50+ departures multiplied by a conservative replacement cost. These are the closest thing he has to survey-based diagnostics.

### 6.8 Budget cycles and timing
- **The Quiet Window** (3 Aug): budgets, roles and vendor categories are written in quiet periods (August, December, Ramadan, after a project ends). The quiet gives two advantages: open diaries and cheap attention. Free move: name your next 2 quiet windows and the deadline after each one (budget freeze, season restart, posting cycle, statutory date). "A quiet window without a deadline is a holiday." "I build in the quiet and collect in the loud."
- Specific dates:
  - US 2027 plan designs freeze from August to early October.
  - UK Employment Rights Act duties start in October.
  - The GCC business season restarts on 15 September.
  - Offsites: a Chief of Staff picks the venue for a Q1 offsite in early December.
- **Compliance calendar** (31 Jul): one page covering 12 months and every statutory date that touches workforce health (EU AI Act dates, UK duties, local heat rules, menopause bills). "Every date is a funded deadline with a named owner."
- **Trend windows:** naming-to-locked used to take 3 years and now takes about 3 quarters. A new category's window is 12 to 18 months. The 3-question test: has an authority named it, is capital visible, and can you position yourself credibly within 90 days? "Treat 'not urgent yet' as the buy signal." Move with 70% certainty.
- **Buy signals for healthspan:** the retirement age moved, a menopause or age-inclusion policy was published, senior attrition is rising. "One signal is a warm door; two is an open door."
- **Vendor count heuristic** (1 Oct): count a company's wellness vendors via LinkedIn, its careers page and Glassdoor. More than 5 means they have the problem and are "leaking cash," and an in-house lead becomes justifiable.

### 6.9 Offsites versus retreats (7 Sep)
- "Companies do not buy wellness, they buy an offsite that puts the whole team in a room, and what is waiting on arrival decides the venue." The programme is the thing that wins the venue decision.
- Three problems hide behind every offsite:
  - A Zoom team that must arrive able to decide.
  - A tired leadership layer: sleep, food and recovery between sessions.
  - A sales floor whose state when the number is announced matters.
- The trap is selling a "corporate wellness retreat" to HR. The signers are the Chief of Staff, COO, CRO or Founder.
- Entrepreneurs buy over coffee; companies need a reason on paper and 3 signatures.
- The roomless version (22 Jul): stop "I run guided wellness walks"; start "I run team recovery experiences that get people off their screens and back into the same physical space." The formats are guided walks, cold-water mornings, forest bathing, sunrise mobility, walk-and-talk coaching, and food-and-movement half-days. "The venue is borrowed, the transformation is yours."
- Social prescribing (25 Sep): "sell a hiking or rucking session to a company and turn it into a mental health intervention." Awareness programmes are losing renewals; "doing" (movement, connection, agency) is winning them.
- Market data:
  - Corporate retreats: $31.8B (2024), heading to $73.7B (2034).
  - Business travel: $1.71T.
  - Wellness tourism: $830B, heading to $1.35T.

### 6.10 Wellness real estate and spaces (20 Jul)
- The sector grew 19.5%/yr, the fastest of all sectors, and carries a 10 to 25% price premium.
- "A building can be finished, a wellness program cannot be poured." Each wellness district needs 5 operating disciplines: clinic, spa/recovery, movement, nutrition, and a resident health calendar.
- There are two lines to sell into:
  - **Design-stage capex:** write the "health programming brief": what the spaces are for, what outcomes they deliver and how they are measured. This makes you a design-stage consultant.
  - **Post-handover opex:** a management contract, "the most durable revenue in this field."
- Stop "I offer wellness coaching and workshops." Start "I design and operate the health programming that delivers your wellness premium."
- He notes that wellness real estate does require some accreditations.

### 6.11 Packaging and new-market positioning
- **Import Play:** carry a category that is proven abroad into your market. "The budget line abroad tells you the budget line at home." Line: "Already funded in the US and UK. I run it here."
- **Concentration versus compounding markets:** in mature markets you make a displacement pitch (slow, reference-heavy, won on specifics). In fast-growing ones you make a creation pitch (relationship-heavy, won on vocabulary).
- **Sell twice:** the same expertise goes to a company as risk reduction and to a consumer as peace of mind.
- **Human premium:** AI does "column one" (programmes, decks, research, generic assessments). You sell "column two": choosing which intervention to fund, facing a skeptical Head of Facilities, telling the CFO which number moves, being in the room. "If your service can be recorded once and sold repeatedly, you're in a declining market."
- **5 roles:** a corporate wellness lead who owns a number, a retest translator, a cohort facilitator, a manager load advisor, and a duty-of-care advisor.
- **Three skills that get you hired and keep you there** (6 Oct): read a budget, get other departments to say yes, and pick one number and move it every month ("No number, no parking space next year").

## 8. Top 10 takeaways for an Israeli corporate wellness and resilience consultant (LinkedIn, Hebrew)

1. **Lead every post and proposal with the budget line, not the modality.** Adapt "What budget line does this sit on?" to the Israeli context: absence days, attrition, reserve-duty (miluim) return-to-work, manager burnout, insurance/claims. Rewrite the LinkedIn headline from "breathing and cold-exposure instructor" to a problem plus an outcome, such as protecting manager capacity in high-pressure teams. Run his test: "What do you think I sell?" Adopt the "capacity protection" vocabulary.
2. **Sell to the manager layer, not "all employees."** The Gallup manager collapse (31% to 22%, 70% of variance) is the strongest proof point. Package a "pilot for 20 managers" with a load audit (your burnout survey) and report it against one cost line. This fits resilience and breathing work naturally.
3. **Replace ROI with directional trend reporting.** Offer a 12-week paid pilot with a control team, weekly leading indicators (sleep, energy, burnout-survey score) and one lagging cost metric. For retainers, add a 3-year trend commitment on attrition cost, absence and productivity hours. This is your ROI/VOI story without overclaiming.
4. **Build a four-step price ladder and quote ranges.** The steps are a paid diagnostic (survey plus 2-page findings), then a pilot, then a retainer or PEPM programme, then a design or strategy day. Never quote a single hourly or per-lecture number. Use his Range Return structure and end on "what can you approve without asking anyone else?"
5. **Never send free proposals or free assessments.** Turn the "discovery" into a paid diagnostic with the next step written on the same page. Use questions about the cost of the opportunity, of delay and of doing nothing as the free value.
6. **Reposition retreats as offsites and cold or breathing work as "team recovery."** Target the CEO, COO, Chief of Staff and sales leaders who already have offsite budgets, and venues and event companies as channel partners. Pitch "how people arrive, sleep and recover, and what that does to day-two decisions." Heat-season readiness, operational resilience and a duty-of-care file for field, ops, security and logistics teams map directly onto your cold, breath and resilience IP.
7. **Wellness spaces = design-stage brief + operating contract.** Sell an outcome-and-measurement "health programming brief" to companies building or redesigning offices and to developers. Follow it with ongoing content programming as the recurring line.
8. **Use his content engine, not his words.** Run 3 fixed weekly themes (market/data, positioning/career, selling to companies). Each post follows the same skeleton: a personal scene, the misread, 3 sourced numbers, a budget translation, a "stop saying / start saying" swap, and one 10-minute free action. Translate the hook formulas (contrarian, "they don't buy X they buy Y," the buyer quote as headline, the confession one-liner) into Hebrew.
9. **Mine your existing B2C and workshop clients for B2B doors.** Use the Bridge Question ("who owns that problem at your company? not asking for an intro") and a 24-hour follow-up page. Also map the gatekeepers: ask "who do you check with?" Get onto procurement and supplier portals the same day, and court insurance agents and brokers who manage group policies.
10. **Use the funnel mechanics that create two-way engagement.** Use a one-letter reply segmentation (A/B/C), a diagnostic, a lead magnet with "hit reply," and a community. A Hebrew WhatsApp group or LinkedIn group plays the role of his Skool group. Turn real reader and client questions into short Q&A posts, and publish a small proprietary survey (for example "N Israeli HR managers on burnout") as a recurring authority asset. Time big pushes to Israeli budget windows: Q4 for next-year budgets, plus the quiet periods after the holidays and in summer.

**Caveats:**
- Some of his advice contradicts itself. Manager work goes on L&D (17 Jul) versus "dies as leadership development, put it on health cost" (18 Sep). His survey labels also shift between issues.
- The price urgency ($149, $109, then back to $94, then $47) is heavy-handed. Model the cadence, not the pressure.
- Most of his cost data is US-centric; localise the numbers (for example Israeli health-tax and insurance structures, Bituach Leumi data).
